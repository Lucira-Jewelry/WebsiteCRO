Measurement discrepancy found during today's (4 Aug) re-test: re-running cro_fold_analysis just now reproduced the same 13,220px / 16.3-screen figure above — stable. But a separate tool, cro_heatmap's full-page screenshot capture on the identical URL and device, came back 26,440px tall — almost exactly double. The extra height is real content (a "Story Behind the Product" block, a store-locator, a reviews carousel, "Our Process", a lab-grown-vs-mined comparison table, and an "Explore Our Range" strip) that cro_fold_analysis's DOM-height read doesn't count, likely because cro_heatmap's slower full-page scroll triggers more lazy-loaded sections than a single settle wait does. Neither number is wrong — they're measuring different things: 16.3 screens / 13,220px is what a typical scrolling visitor's browser reports as the document at rest (the number used for the 17.9% reach line above), while 26,440px is closer to everything technically on the page once every lazy section has loaded. Worth knowing both exist before quoting "the page is X screens tall" as a single fact.

Recommendations

What to do next, by device

Mobile

  1. Treat T3 (reviews summary near the top) as higher priority than its "Critical, open" status alone suggests — with ~80% new users, the page has one shot to establish trust, and reviews are the strongest trust asset currently unpublished at 47–48% depth.
  2. 1.18 pages/session suggests visitors aren't exploring further after landing — consider a related-products prompt once T3 and T21 are resolved, not before.

Desktop

  1. Fix T21 first, above everything else on this page — a 176.7s-average session (Paramount) is exactly the profile the ~17s LCP actively damages. This isn't a marginal user; it's someone already spending 3 minutes on the page.
  2. Lower new-user share (32–68%) than mobile means desktop PDP traffic is warmer on average — speed, not trust signals, is the higher-leverage fix here.

This one doesn't fit the homepage/PLP pattern. On PDP, Android and iOS click almost identically — both dominated by legitimate variant selection ("Yellow" metal, size codes, "CUSTOMIZE"), popup barely present on either (Android 3 rage clicks, iOS 0). Yet iOS bounces 18 points higher and engages 18 points lower, with no rage-click signature to explain it. The leading hypothesis: the iOS Meta in-app WebView JS error found on the Traffic & Meta/Ads page (window.webkit.messageHandlers undefined, 817 sessions) silently breaks something on PDP for iOS visitors arriving via Instagram/Facebook — a script crash wouldn't produce a rage click, just a quietly broken page. Worth testing directly (T17) rather than assuming it's the popup.

Both rendered on the same emulated mobile viewport (iPhone UA) — no separate Android device profile exists in the measurement layer.
