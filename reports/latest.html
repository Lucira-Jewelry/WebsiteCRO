Nobody reads the reviews on a ₹44,560 ring

Reviews sit 6,536 px down a 15,804 px page — the eighth screen of nineteen. The average visitor stops at 2,886 px. For a high-consideration purchase where social proof is one of the strongest conversion levers available, it is functionally not on the page. Moving reviews (or a compact rating summary) to just below Product Details at ~2,500 px puts them inside the range visitors actually reach, and requires no new content.

Measured live at 375×812 on 27 Jul. Sticky price/ATC returned negative offsets, meaning they're pinned to the viewport and always visible — that part of the PDP works well.

What the pattern means

  • Every PDP clusters at 18–21%Remarkably tight. This isn't content-specific disinterest — it's a structural consequence of PDPs being 15,000–16,000 px long. Visitors aren't abandoning early; the pages are simply far longer than anyone scrolls.
  • The cart is the exception at 80.9%The one page people scroll almost fully. Combined with 27.5 s engagement, 2.8% bounce and 538 rage clicks, this is the signature of someone hunting for a way to proceed — not someone losing interest.
  • Homepage and PLPs sit at 26–28%Better than PDPs but still means roughly three-quarters of each page is unseen.
  • Implication for merchandisingAnything placed below ~20% of a PDP or ~28% of a PLP should be treated as unpublished until proven otherwise.

Paid Social bounces at 62–100% on every page it touches

/collections/rings is the control: 33% bounce from Organic Search with 582 s sessions, and 100% bounce from Paid Social with 5.8 s sessions — the same page, same week. The all- collections look bad only because they are where Meta traffic lands; roughly 1,800 of all-rings' and all-earrings' sessions come from paid or organic social.

This ties directly to the in-app browser evidence: the top JavaScript errors on the site are Meta WebView signatures (window.webkit.messageHandlers undefined — 817 sessions; Java object is gone — 694). The likeliest reading is that a large share of Paid Social traffic never gets a working page at all. Redesigning the landing page would not have fixed this.

Method

Caveats

  • Screenshots were not capturable in this environment — the browser pane could not composite frames. The fold analysis substitutes live DOM measurement at 375×812, which yields exact pixel positions rather than a visual impression. Popups are conditionally triggered and did not fire for a fresh session, so they were identified from Clarity's click stream rather than observed directly.
  • Clicked-text weights aggregate on a different basis than per-page click totals; use them as a ranking, not a count.
  • Blank cells in the click matrix mean "not in Clarity's top-10 response", not a confirmed zero.
  • Bounce rate on cart, shipping and payment is misleading — GA4 bounce is session-scoped and reads near-zero there because users engage before abandoning. Step abandonment is the correct measure and is shown alongside.
  • Small samples: /collections/rings Paid Social is n=10 and desktop payment n=10. Both are directional, though the Paid Social pattern is corroborated across four pages and ~2,800 sessions.
  • Fold analysis is one PDP. Scroll depth clusters tightly at 18–21% across all twelve top PDPs, which suggests the template generalises, but only Corinthian was measured directly.
