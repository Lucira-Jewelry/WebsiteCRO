/search · GA4 property 478308692, 30d window (13 Jul–11 Aug) unless noted · Clarity 7d (5–11 Aug) · live checks + Shopify catalog cross-reference 11 Aug 2026
Job of this page

Take a visitor who already knows what they want and get them to that exact product. Search traffic is a small slice of sessions but the highest-intent segment on the site — it converts far above the sitewide baseline where the underlying search actually works. Where it doesn't (see the confirmed defect below), it converts at exactly zero.

Search_Click is not the same journey as view_search_results. It fires on 61 different pages — mostly the homepage (142) and collection pages (eterna 95, eternity-rings 63, jewelry 45, bestsellers 31) — and only 16 of its 606 firings happen on /search itself. A funnel report requiring Search_Click to be directly followed by view_search_results converts only 2 of 281 users. Read it as "opened the search icon/overlay", a mostly separate action from actually submitting and viewing a query. The real search funnel below starts at view_search_results.

Segmentation

Known vs. unknown users of search

Proxy: GA4 newVsReturning — see caveat

GA4's Data API does not expose sign-in status (signInStatus is BigQuery-export-only, confirmed by testing it here — the API rejects it as an invalid dimension). The closest available "known vs. unknown" split is newVsReturning: known = returning visitor (this device/browser has been seen before), unknown = new/first-time visitor. This is device recognition, not authenticated login — a logged-in customer on a new device would still show as "new". Treat the numbers below as directional, not identity-verified.

Every search-driven purchase in the last 30 days came from a known (returning) visitor. Zero came from a new visitor, despite new visitors supplying 29% of all search-results views (71 of 245) and 15.8% of result clicks (19 of 120). New-visitor search drops from 71 → 19 → 2 → 1 → 0: it survives to add-to-cart at roughly a third the rate of returning visitors (2.8% vs 25.3% of entrants) and never closes. Search currently behaves like a repeat-customer tool, not a conversion path for people discovering Lucira for the first time.

Sitewide funnel: view_item 244,822 → add_to_cart 7,308 → begin_checkout 714 → purchase 51 (30d, users). The entry-point comparison (search-results-view vs. product-view) isn't apples-to-apples — search entrants are already a qualified, intent-filtered population — but the add-to-cart and checkout-completion rates are directly comparable and both come out clearly in search's favour. This is a protect-and-invest signal: search visitors who reach a real result buy at several times the site average.

Per-term revenue/add-to-cart cannot be pulled from the GA4 Data API. Every term below returns addToCarts, ecommercePurchases and purchaseRevenue as a flat zero when queried alongside searchTerm — including terms independently confirmed to convert. This is an API-scope limitation (searchTerm doesn't join to session/ecommerce metrics through this endpoint, unlike GA4 UI's discontinued Site Search report), not a real "nobody buys after searching X" signal. A true per-term revenue view needs the BigQuery export.

Live-result verification (the "Live result" column) covers the 35 terms with ≥4 sessions — every one of them was reloaded in a real browser with a full AJAX settle wait. This storefront is a JS-rendered SPA: a raw HTTP fetch of a search URL returns the page shell before results hydrate and can misreport a working query as "0 Results" (caught mid-audit on "Pendants", which read 0 on first fetch and 93 on a proper reload) — so anything checked without a render-and-wait step isn't trustworthy and isn't reported here as confirmed. The remaining 658 terms (2–3 sessions and the 557-strong one-session tail) are shown with GA4's numbers but marked not checked — that's 51% of all search sessions by volume, mostly single-occurrence typos, SKU guesses and rare phrasings, not verified live in this pass.

Critical

The confirmed defect: a taxonomy mismatch zeroes out entire categories

Lucira's #1 search term by volume ("classic diamond nose pin", 75 sessions/60d) has returned zero results on every query variant tested — not because the product doesn't exist, but because the storefront auto-applies a category filter whose value doesn't match the Shopify catalog.

What the site does

Any query containing "nose pin" silently appends &filter.p.product_type=Nose+Pins to the URL — even when the user (or this audit) never requested a filter. Any query containing "mangalsutra" appends &filter.p.product_type=Mangalsutras. Both are auto-added client-side and cannot be removed by the shopper.

What the catalog actually has

Shopify's real product_type values are Nosepins (one word) and Mangalsutra (singular) — confirmed via the Admin API. At least 10 active nose-pin products are in stock, including "Classic Round Diamond Nosepin" (9 units, ₹5,999) — a near-exact match for the broken term — and 5+ active Mangalsutra pieces. The filter value and the catalog value simply don't agree, so the intersection is always empty.

Recommendations

What to do next

Fix now

  1. Correct the two confirmed filter/catalog mismatches — point the "nose pin" auto-filter at Nosepins and the "mangalsutra" auto-filter at Mangalsutra. This alone recovers at least ~85 sessions/60d (nose-pin family) + ~8 sessions/60d (mangalsutra family) from a guaranteed dead end, on a term that's currently the single highest-volume query on the site.
  2. Audit the full auto-filter mapping table against live product_type values — this was found from spot-checking 5 categories and 2 broke. There is no reason to assume those are the only two.
  3. Stop re-suggesting a broken term as a "Trending Search" chip on its own zero-results page — at minimum, suppress trending-search suggestions that themselves resolve to 0 results.

Investigate next

  1. Search converts new (first-time) visitors at roughly a third the rate of returning visitors and closes zero purchases from them in 30 days. Worth a qualitative look at whether search UI assumes brand familiarity (e.g. SKU-style/internal naming in results) that a first-time visitor wouldn't have.
  2. Get BigQuery export access to unlock true per-search-term revenue/add-to-cart — the Data API cannot join searchTerm to ecommerce outcomes, so today's term list has no reliable conversion column.
  3. Spot-check the remaining un-tested top terms (kids Earrings, tn0031, Chain, Rings) the same way, to size the taxonomy-mismatch problem beyond the two confirmed categories.
