What the live comparison above shows

Engagement rate makes the channel story sharper than bounce rate alone: across every range in the filter above, all-rings' engagement rate runs roughly half of eterna's on the identical template — at the default 30-day view that's 33.0% vs 64.2%. All-rings also consistently skews toward far more new users than eterna — it's absorbing colder, paid traffic. Desktop eterna tracks mobile eterna closely on every range — the template performs identically once you control for channel.

Unlike the homepage, iOS is slightly ahead of Android here — lower bounce, higher engagement. Android's clicks are still popup-heavy ("Register to Win" 1,673, "Spin the Wheel" 792) alongside legitimate collection browsing ("Eterna" 3,494), driving 114 rage clicks. iOS barely touches the popup ("Spin the Wheel" only 19) and clicks mostly on real content — a promo banner, the "Yellow" metal filter, "Sort". Same pattern as the homepage (Android fights the popup, iOS mostly ignores it) but here it isn't costing iOS anything on bounce/engagement.

Both rendered on the same emulated mobile viewport (iPhone UA) — no separate Android device profile exists in the measurement layer.

Recommendations

What to do next, by device

Mobile

  1. Don't optimise all-rings' layout or copy to chase its low 33.0% engagement rate — the gap to eterna's 64.2% is traffic composition (58.9% new/paid users), not the page. Fix belongs on the Traffic & Meta/Ads page, not here.
  2. Maintain the current template on organic-heavy collections (eterna, rings, jewelry) — nothing here is under-performing once channel is accounted for.

Desktop

  1. No action needed — engagement rate, session duration and CLS (0.004–0.005, corrected from the earlier 0.154) are all healthy and match mobile's organic-traffic numbers closely.
