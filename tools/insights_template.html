Lucira Jewelry

Generated 28 Jul 2026 Analytics: 27 May – 26 Jul 2026 · Behaviour: 12–26 Jul · Lab: measured live 28 Jul Online storefront only — 127 ERP/store orders excluded

Previous runs identified where the funnel leaked using GA4, Clarity and Shopify. This run adds a Playwright measurement layer that drove the real site at both breakpoints — so the cause is no longer inferred from click counts. It is on record, with screenshots, and it is one thing.

The Finding

Checkout is an OTP wall with no guest path — and mobile renders it as a full-screen takeover

Root cause · proven live · fix is a layout change

Clicking “Proceed to Checkout” does not go to checkout. It demands a phone number.

Playwright added a real ₹44,370 ring to the cart and clicked the checkout button on both breakpoints. On both, the URL stayed at /checkout/cart and a login modal appeared asking for a phone number and an OTP. There is no guest checkout option on either device.

The gate is identical. The experience of it is not — and that difference is the entire mobile-versus-desktop conversion gap.

Compounding

A lottery wheel greets the first page of every mobile session

It fires on the session’s first page — which for paid traffic is the landing page

A fresh session on the homepage, a collection page or a product page all produced the same 100%-viewport overlay at z-index 9999. It appears once per session, so it lands on whatever page the visitor arrives on. Since most paid traffic lands deep on a PLP or PDP, that means the very first thing a paid visitor sees is a lottery wheel demanding their phone number.

Two gaps worth naming

Reviews are unreachable. They sit at 47.8% of a 19.5-screen page; the median visitor stops at 18.3%. For a ₹44,370 considered purchase, the single strongest trust asset is functionally not on the page. Moving them to just under Product Details (~16%) requires no new content.

No EMI or financing anywhere. Across every page and both breakpoints, no financing, EMI or instalment messaging matched. For ₹44,000–₹1,85,000 baskets in the Indian market that is a notable omission — EMI availability is a standard purchase enabler at this price point.

Caveat on the second: this is a keyword match against rendered text. If financing is offered under wording my patterns didn't cover, or only appears inside the gated checkout, it would read as absent. Worth a human confirmation before acting.

The origin and CDN are fine. TTFB is 95–181 ms and lab LCP is 0.7–2.4 s — all inside “good”. The field median is roughly 1.5–5× slower, which points at real-user devices and networks, not at server response.

That reframes the recommendation: speed work belongs in payload weight and render path — image sizes, third-party script count, main-thread work — not in origin or hosting. Notably, the site loads 14 third-party script hosts, including two separate popup/engagement SDKs.

Recommendations

What to change, in order — and how

Ordered by expected impact per unit of effort. Revenue figures are modelled against the real 84-order online baseline (₹21,41,269/month, AOV ₹25,491) using conservative relative lifts — they are scenarios, not forecasts.

  1. Make the mobile checkout modal behave like the desktop one

    CriticalEffort: hours+₹4–6L / month modelled

    The cheapest high-impact change available. Desktop converts 4.2× better through the identical gate purely because the cart, the total and the CART→SHIPPING→PAYMENT progress stay visible. Reproduce that on mobile as a bottom sheet covering ~60% of the viewport instead of a full takeover.

    How
    • Change the mobile modal from full-viewport to a bottom sheet; keep the cart summary and step indicator visible above it.
    • Retitle it to state the purpose — “Verify your number to continue to shipping”, not “Welcome to Lucira”.
    • Show the order total inside the sheet so the user knows what they're logging in for.
    • Enlarge the close affordance and give it a visible hit area of at least 44×44 px.
    • Verify with cro_funnel_walk at the mobile breakpoint before and after.

  2. Offer guest checkout — collect the phone number at the shipping step instead

    CriticalEffort: days+₹8–12L / month modelled

    The larger prize. A mandatory OTP before checkout asks for commitment before the visitor has seen shipping cost or delivery date. You already capture a phone number at the shipping step — the pre-checkout gate is redundant. Nothing is lost by moving it: the number still arrives, one step later, from a user who has decided to buy.

    How
    • Add “Continue as guest” as the primary action; keep login secondary for returning customers.
    • Capture phone at shipping, where it is contextually justified by delivery.
    • Instrument it: fire begin_checkout when checkout opens, not after OTP succeeds — the current placement is why GA4 shows only 144.
    • Run as an A/B test if possible; if not, ship to mobile only first and compare against desktop as the control.

  3. Suppress the spin-wheel modal on entry — trigger it on exit intent or after real engagement

    CriticalEffort: config only+₹3–5L / month modelled

    A paid visitor's first impression is a lottery wheel demanding a phone number, on the page they paid to reach. Homepage popups absorb 2,221 clicks against 215 on the top navigation link — a 10.3 : 1 ratio.

    How
    • In NitroCommerce (nitro-popup-sdk.js): never show on the first page view of a session.
    • Trigger on exit intent, or after 2+ page views or 30+ seconds — a visitor who has browsed is worth asking.
    • Exclude /checkout/* entirely so it can never compound with the login gate.
    • Re-audit with cro_overlay_audit against a fresh session on home, a PLP and a PDP.

  4. Move Customer Reviews from 47.8% to roughly 16% page depth

    HighEffort: hoursNo new content

    Reviews sit on screen 9 of 19.5; the median visitor stops at screen 3.6. The strongest trust asset for a ₹44,370 purchase is effectively unpublished. Certification, returns and shipping are already well placed — reviews are the one real casualty.

    How
    • Put a compact rating summary (stars, count, one quoted review) directly beneath Product Details.
    • Leave the full review list where it is; only the summary needs to move up.
    • Confirm the new depth with cro_fold_analysis at avgScrollDepthPct: 18.3.

  5. Fix the Meta traffic path before spending more on it

    HighEffort: days₹52,336 / month confirmed waste

    Paid Social bounces at 62–100% on every page, including pages that convert well from other channels — so this is not a landing-page problem. Three campaigns spent ₹52,336 in 30 days with zero attributed conversions.

    How
    • Open the full funnel inside the Instagram and Facebook in-app browsers on a real device and complete a purchase. The WebView JS errors suggest it may not be completable.
    • Pause the three zero-conversion campaigns (Display retargeting, Demand Gen prospecting, Noida GMB Search) pending the tracking fix.
    • Add bot filtering at the GA4 data-stream level — 34% of Clarity sessions were bot-flagged.

  6. Fix the Google Ads conversion configuration

    HighEffort: minutesUnblocks all measurement

    Campaign “conversions” currently blend Purchase + Begin Checkout + two UPLOAD_CLICKS actions, inflating totals roughly 7× against real orders. The correctly wired GA4 purchase import is excluded from the bidding metric — so Smart Bidding is optimising toward the noisy signal and ignoring the clean one.

    How
    • Set Begin Checkout to not count in the Conversions column.
    • Confirm whether Offline Purchase and CRM Online Conversion are intentional O2O attribution before changing them — for an omnichannel business they may be deliberate.
    • Switch the primary bidding action to Lucira (web) purchase (the GA4 import).
    • Grant the read_pixels scope so the Web Pixel config can be inspected, or check Settings → Customer events manually.

  7. Shorten the mobile page templates

    MediumEffort: weeks

    The homepage is 21.5 screens and the PDP 19.5, against a scroll ceiling of 18–28%. Roughly three-quarters of both pages is never seen. Anything below ~20% of a PDP or ~28% of the homepage should be treated as unpublished until proven otherwise.

    How
    • Audit what sits below the reach line and either promote it or delete it — do not add more.
    • The desktop homepage is 16.4 screens against 40% scroll, so it is proportionally far healthier; mobile is the priority.
    • Reduce the 14 third-party script hosts — two separate popup/engagement SDKs are loaded.

  8. Confirm whether EMI / financing is offered, and surface it if so

    MediumEffort: hours if it exists

    No financing messaging matched on any page at either breakpoint, on baskets of ₹44,000–₹1,85,000. If EMI is available, it belongs beside the price. If it isn't, it is worth evaluating as a conversion lever at this price point in this market.

    How
    • Confirm with the payments team first — this is a keyword match and could be a false negative.
    • If offered, show “from ₹X/month” beside the sticky price, which is already always visible.

Method

Limits of this run

  • Lab vitals are single-run per page. Two of eight readings were clear outliers (desktop PDP at 19 s). The cro_vitals tool medians across three runs; the batch capture did not. Treat individual lab LCP figures as indicative and re-run before acting on any one of them.
  • Desktop “never seen” flags are unreliable in the batch capture — it applied the mobile scroll depth to desktop pages. The desktop rows in the fold table use Clarity's 40.1% desktop figure; the batch JSON does not.
  • The overlay detector false-positives on tall content sections. The desktop PDP flagged 116.7% coverage for a “Fast Shipping / Trending / Similar Items” block, which is content, not an overlay. The z-index 9999 Authentication modal is the real finding; verify any coverage figure over 100% against its screenshot.
  • Automated modal dismissal failed once, on the collection page — the heuristic matched an unrelated element. This is a tool limitation, not evidence that the close button is broken; the screenshots show a working ✕. The usability concern is that it is small and competing with a busy graphic, not that it fails.
  • “Spin the Wheel” never resolved to a text node, so the site's second-most-clicked element is absent from the heatmap images. Popup pressure in those images is a floor.
  • EMI absence is a keyword match against rendered text and could be a false negative. Confirm with the payments team before treating it as a gap.
  • Lab measurements are one machine, one network, one moment. They prove the gate exists and is reproducible; they do not describe the distribution of real-user experience. Clarity remains the field source.
